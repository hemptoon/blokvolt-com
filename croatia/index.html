EV charging in Croatia

Croatia's electric-car market is small but grew quickly in 2026: Eurostat counts about 12,600 battery-electric passenger cars on the road at the end of 2025, and ACEA reports 1,781 new BEVs in January–June 2026, about four and a half times the same period of 2025. Public charging is run by 32 companies, led by HEP's ELEN network, Petrol and Hrvatski Telekom, with published prices of about €0.35–0.42/kWh for AC and €0.66–0.78/kWh for fast DC without a subscription. Home wallboxes are sold by solar and electrical firms, the supplier E.ON, online shops and a DIY chain at roughly €475–1,500 for the unit; in apartment buildings, installing charging points is a co-owners' decision taken by a majority of ownership shares under the building-management law in force since 2025.

Charging in an apartment building

What the law says about a charger in a shared garage or parking lot — who has to agree, who pays for the electricity and how it is metered.

Who decides in a multi-unit building

Decisions on installing ducting and charging points for electric vehicles, 'vodeći računa o sigurnosti u slučaju požara' (with regard to fire safety), are listed among building-management decisions. Such a decision is adopted when co-owners holding more than 50% of the co-ownership shares vote for it; a qualified majority is reserved for other listed decisions.

Law: Zakon o upravljanju i održavanju zgrada (NN 152/24, 47/26), Art. 39(1), 39(3) item 10 and 39(4); in force since 01.01.2025

Source: Zakon.hr – Zakon o upravljanju i održavanju zgrada · consolidated text NN 152/24, 47/26; checked 23.09.2026 · high reliability
Fire safety

'Na mjestima za punjenje električnih vozila u zgradama ili na zgradama potrebno je primijeniti mjere zaštite od požara' – fire-protection measures must be applied at charging points in or on buildings.

Law: Zakon o upravljanju i održavanju zgrada, Art. 20(7)

Source: Zakon.hr – Zakon o upravljanju i održavanju zgrada · consolidated text NN 152/24, 47/26; checked 23.09.2026 · high reliability
Single owner acting alone

No provision letting one co-owner install a charger in common parts without a co-owners' decision was found in the consolidated text; the law deals with charging points through the decision rule above.

Law: Zakon o upravljanju i održavanju zgrada

Source: Zakon.hr – Zakon o upravljanju i održavanju zgrada · consolidated text NN 152/24, 47/26; checked 23.09.2026 · medium reliability
Costs of common parts

Costs of the building's common parts are shared among co-owners 'razmjerno njihovim suvlasničkim dijelovima' (in proportion to their co-ownership shares).

Law: Zakon o upravljanju i održavanju zgrada, Art. 28

Source: Zakon.hr – Zakon o upravljanju i održavanju zgrada · consolidated text NN 152/24, 47/26; checked 23.09.2026 · high reliability
Electricity and metering in practice

In a reported case at a new apartment building in Zagreb, the owner ran a 40 m three-phase cable from his apartment through conduit laid by the developer to his outdoor parking space and connected the charger to his own apartment meter; he said no paperwork was needed ('Od papira za to ne treba apsolutno ništa'). He also said fire inspectors were refusing chargers in underground garages although, in his view, no rule requires this.

In August 2026 a draft 'Pravilnik o građevinama i radovima za koje nije potrebna građevinska dozvola' was put to public consultation; critics fear it could require extra design documentation and a longer procedure for home wallboxes. The draft was not final.

Building-level charging services

E-Punjači (TSG Croatia d.o.o.) states that shared parking in apartment buildings needs smart stations with online access to control and split costs, with access rights per user, metering of 'točan utrošak svakog pojedinog korisnika' and automated billing. No company advertising a subscription charging service for residential buildings was found (re-charge.hr could not be checked).

Law: —

EU rules (EPBD recast)

The recast Energy Performance of Buildings Directive (EU) 2024/1275 calls for removing barriers to recharging infrastructure 'such as split incentives and administrative complications' (recital 50). Whether Croatia has transposed its building-charging provisions was not confirmed in this check.

Law: Directive (EU) 2024/1275

Alternative-fuels infrastructure law

Croatia's law on alternative-fuels infrastructure deals with publicly accessible charging; for non-public charging points it only says the national policy framework may include measures to encourage and ease their installation.

National plans and EU rules

  • Under the EU Alternative Fuels Infrastructure Regulation (AFIR, (EU) 2023/1804), Croatia must provide publicly accessible charging with a total power of at least 1.3 kW per registered battery-electric car or van and 0.80 kW per plug-in hybrid (Art. 3(1)). Source: eur-lex.europa.eu · Regulation (EU) 2023/1804 · high reliability
  • On the TEN-T core network, AFIR requires charging pools at most 60 km apart with at least 400 kW in total by 31.12.2025 and 600 kW by 31.12.2027, each with at least one 150 kW point (Art. 3). Source: eur-lex.europa.eu · Regulation (EU) 2023/1804 · high reliability
  • AFIR requires ad hoc payment without a contract at all public charging points, and card payment at points of 50 kW or more installed from 13.04.2024; existing ones on the TEN-T network must follow by 01.01.2027 (Art. 5). Source: eur-lex.europa.eu · Regulation (EU) 2023/1804 · high reliability
  • Croatia's 2017 national policy framework for alternative fuels (NN 34/2017) set minimum targets of 602 public charging sockets at 348 stations by 2025 (434 AC, 168 DC of 50 kW+) and 806 sockets at 479 stations by 2030 (554 AC, 252 DC); whether these targets have been updated was not checked. Source: alternative-fuels-observatory.ec.europa.eu · adopted 06.04.2017 (NN 34/2017) · high reliability
