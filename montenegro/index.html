EV charging in Montenegro

Montenegro's EV market is small but growing: Monstat counted 950 electric-only and 5,874 hybrid vehicles among 322,241 registered motor vehicles in 2025 (715 and 3,221 a year earlier), and battery-electric cars made up 1.8% of new car registrations. Public charging is sparse and mostly slow: a draft national framework counts 94 publicly accessible charging points with none above 150 kW, there are no Tesla Superchargers, and none of the operators checked publishes per-kWh prices. Only a few firms advertise home chargers and none publishes prices, while household electricity averaged 9.98 euro cents per kWh including taxes in 2025.

National plans and EU rules

  • Under the EU AFIR rules, charging points on TEN-T corridors must be at most 60 km apart; Montenegro's draft National Policy Framework for alternative fuels infrastructure (Ministry of Transport with the Ministry of Energy) says at least 14 car-charging locations along the TEN-T network and 11 truck-charging hubs (27 MW in total) are needed.Source: borba.me · 16.06.2026 · medium reliability
  • The framework, Montenegro's first aligned with AFIR (Regulation (EU) 2023/1804), is EU-funded and prepared by a consortium led by DAI Global Belgium with GIZ and ENOVA; it was presented at a stakeholder workshop in Podgorica on 16.09.2026.Source: evropskakuca.me · 15.09.2026 · high reliability
  • A dedicated Law on Alternative Fuels Infrastructure is being prepared, with adoption by Parliament expected by the end of 2026; the 2030 goal is about 35,000 electric passenger cars with about 50 fast and 500 slow chargers.Source: rtcg.me · 22.07.2026 · medium reliability
  • The draft framework also proposes a single national portal for permits with legally fixed maximum decision times, targeted financial incentives for charge-point operators, and minimum user rights, including ad hoc charging without a prior contract.Source: borba.me · 16.06.2026 · medium reliability

What we could not verify

Gaps we know about. If you can fill one with a public source, write to hello@blokvolt.com.

  • Home chargers: only three Montenegrin sellers/installers could be verified, and none publishes device or installation prices; the only price ranges come from an EV dealer's guide.
  • Car importers: Jadran Auto d.o.o. (Hyundai) has a wallbox information page without models, prices or an installation offer; Toyota Montenegro's charging page links to the Croatian Toyota HomeCharge page; the BMW Voli and MINI home-charging pages returned 404.
  • Checked without an EV-charger offer: retailers Tehno Planet and Tehnomax; electrical wholesalers/contractors Electro Group, ENEL PS CG, Telemont and Vigoris Ecotech. Serbian installers Elektropunjači/Charge&GO and ElektroMil (Niš) do not name Montenegro as a service area.
  • Did not load: elvod.me (Elektrovod, DNS error), popovicelektrosistem.co.me (certificate error), evcmontenegro.me/products/ (server error 500), paragraf.me (403), pvportal.me (401), epcg.com tariff pages (404; the site renders via JavaScript).
  • Public charging prices: no operator price list found for EKO/Jugopetrol, EVC Montenegro, Orion eMobility or the motorway chargers; the operator of the motorway chargers (Pelev Brijeg, Gornje Mrke) is not named in any source.
  • Payment by bank card directly at a Montenegrin charger is not confirmed; coverage of Montenegrin chargers by roaming apps (Charge&GO, Electroverse, Shell Recharge, Plugsurfing, Chargemap) is not confirmed.
  • Electricity: EPCG's per-kWh prices for the higher and lower tariff were not retrieved; REGAGEN's 2025 energy-sector report (source of the 9.98 c/kWh figure) is not yet posted on regagen.co.me.
  • Registrations: no 2026 year-to-date count of new battery-electric cars was found. Monstat's quarterly releases (Q1 2026 of 14.05.2026, Q2 2026 of 12.08.2026) contain a table by propulsion energy, but its figures could not be extracted reliably and were not used. The annual Monstat release does not split electric-only vehicles into passenger cars and other categories.
  • Subsidies: amounts, budgets and deadlines of the 2026 SKLAD E-mobility rounds (published 27.02.2026 and 15.05.2026) were not confirmed; only their dates and closed status were seen.
  • Apartment buildings: the text of the Law on Housing and Maintenance of Residential Buildings (Zakon o stanovanju i održavanju stambenih zgrada) was not retrieved, so rules on co-owner consent, cost sharing and sub-metering for chargers in shared garages are not confirmed. No building-specific charging rules were found in the consolidated Energy Law text checked, and the distribution operator CEDIS's website shows no guidance on connecting EV chargers.
  • No company offering a building-level (multi-unit residential) charging or metering service was found.
  • Method: the web-search quota ran out during the check; the remaining items were verified by fetching known URLs only.
