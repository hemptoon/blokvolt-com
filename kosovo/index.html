EV charging in Kosovo

Kosovo had 1,457 battery-electric passenger cars at the end of 2025 (Eurostat), 51% more than a year earlier, and imports are rising fast: 780 electric cars came in during January–August 2026, 665 of them from China. Electric cars get no tax break — they pay the same 10% customs duty, 18% VAT and excise as combustion cars. The main public network, ECHARGE, publishes its prices: €0.39–0.49 per kWh on DC and €0.21 (day) / €0.19 (night) on AC. At home, night-rate electricity costs about 4.2 euro cents per kWh with VAT.

Subsidies

No scheme Tax relief for electric vehicles

Electric cars pay 10% customs duty, 18% VAT and excise by age like combustion cars. The Chamber of Commerce has asked to remove the duty and cut VAT to 8%; no decision so far.

Source: Gazeta Express · 27.09.2026 · medium reliability

National plans and EU rules

  • The Energy Strategy 2022–2031 sets no numeric targets for electric vehicles or charging stations. Source: me.rks-gov.net · high reliability
  • Prishtina's 2021 e-taxi licensing requires DC chargers of at least 50 kW, including one public charger per operator. Source: prishtinaonline.com · 02.11.2021 · medium reliability
