Countries

Serbia

7,155 electric cars (Ministry of Interior, April 2026)

About 220 public chargers; 36 state chargers on motorways are free

€5,000 purchase subsidy, applications until 1 December 2026

51 companies in the register of installers and sellers

Montenegro

782 battery-electric cars at the end of 2025 (Eurostat)

94 public charging points, none above 150 kW

2026 purchase-grant rounds closed; no home-charger grant

3 home-charger companies found, none publishes prices

Bosnia and Herzegovina

704 battery-electric cars at the end of 2025 (Eurostat)

No official count of public chargers: published figures range from 78 to about 500

Federation of BiH grant of up to 12,000 KM per car: funds reserved, call paused on 18.09.2026

3 home-charger sellers found, 2 with published prices

North Macedonia

2,047 battery-electric cars at the end of 2025

EVN: 12 fast DC sites and 40+ AC points; 25–40 MKD per kWh

No national purchase or home-charger subsidy found for 2026

7 home-charger sellers, published wallbox prices from €440

Croatia

12,616 battery-electric cars on the road at the end of 2025 (Eurostat)

715 stations in the national registry; about 300 ultra-fast connectors

Citizens' grant of €20 million announced for autumn 2026, now including home chargers

14 home-charger sellers and installers listed

Slovenia

9,203 new battery-electric cars in January–August 2026 (19.9% of new cars)

Just over 4,600 public charging points at almost 1,500 locations

Borzen grants: up to €7,800 per car, 55% (max. €1,000) per home charger

15 home-charger sellers and installers listed

Albania

10,938 battery-electric cars at the end of 2025 (Eurostat), +91% in a year

Public charging: VEGA 20+ sites, PlugoAL 5 × 280 kW; OSHEE plans a national network

New EVs VAT-free per the latest source (sources conflict); no annual vehicle tax

Home electricity 10.2 lek/kWh with VAT up to 700 kWh a month

Kosovo

1,457 battery-electric cars at the end of 2025 (Eurostat)

ECHARGE: 24+ sites in Kosovo and Albania, DC €0.39–0.49 per kWh

No tax relief: 10% customs and 18% VAT, as for petrol and diesel cars

Night-rate home electricity about 4.2 euro cents per kWh

How the data is checked

Public sources only

Operators' price lists and apps, company websites, statistics offices, regulators, funds and law texts. Where only the media or a dealer gives a figure, it is marked as medium or low reliability.

A date on everything

Each figure shows the date on its source and the date it was checked. Markets change quickly; an old price is labelled as old.

Same rules for every company

What a company says on its own site goes into the same columns for everyone. No ratings, no rankings, no paid placements.

Gaps are published

Each country page lists what could not be verified, so you know where the picture is incomplete. Maps come from OpenStreetMap and Open Charge Map and say so.
