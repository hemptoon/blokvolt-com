EV charging in North Macedonia

North Macedonia had 2,047 battery-electric passenger cars at the end of 2025, about 0.33% of all passenger cars, and no national subsidy for buying an electric car or a home charger was found for 2026. Public charging is mostly provided by EVN Macedonia, whose map lists 12 DC fast-charging sites of up to 180 kW (nine at Makpetrol fuel stations) and more than 40 AC points in 20 towns; residents with an EVN card pay 25/28 MKD per kWh (AC/DC) and other drivers 30/40 MKD by bank card, VAT included. Seven sellers of home chargers were found, mostly in Skopje; some publish device prices of about €440–1,590 per wallbox, none publishes a fixed installation price, and no building-level charging service for apartment blocks was found.

Who pays and how it is metered

Two set-ups are described: (1) a cable from the resident's own apartment meter, so charging appears on the resident's own EVN bill and can use the cheap tariff; (2) one or two smart chargers on the building's common meter, with RFID cards and a built-in MID-certified meter, where software records each flat's kWh and the manager or house council receives a monthly report to recover the cost from each user.

National plans and EU rules

  • The Transport Community's November 2025 progress report states that none of the Western Balkan partners has yet transposed the EU Alternative Fuels Infrastructure Regulation (AFIR, 2023/1804), and that Transport Community technical assistance will help them prepare national policy frameworks and identify priority corridors. Source: transport-community.org · November 2025 (published December 2025) · medium reliability
  • The same report names North Macedonia's Ministry of Energy, Mining and Mineral Resources as primarily responsible for alternative-fuels policy, and notes that motor-vehicle tax and registration fees are now linked to measured CO2 emissions (Ministry of Finance). Source: transport-community.org · November 2025 (published December 2025) · medium reliability
